# Research with sources

Use this when the answer depends on facts that may have changed, that you are not sure of, or that
the user will act on (buying, configuring, deciding).

1. Restate the real question in one line, including what "good enough" means (a number, a pick, a list).
2. Search with two or three different phrasings: one plain, one with the exact product or version, one
   that looks for problems ("issue", "regression", "vs").
3. Open the most relevant pages instead of trusting the snippets. Prefer primary sources: official docs,
   release notes, the vendor's own pages, the paper itself.
4. Cross-check every number or claim that matters against a second independent source. When two sources
   disagree, say so and say which you trust and why.
5. Answer first, then the evidence: the conclusion in one or two sentences, then the supporting points
   with their links, then what remains uncertain.
6. Date-sensitive facts get the date of the source ("as of the 2026-09 release notes").

Never invent a source, a quote or a number. If the search found nothing useful, say that plainly and
answer from what you know, marked as not verified.
